# Audience avatars — who mikekilcoyne.com is built for

Companion to [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md). CLAUDE.md says *how* the site behaves; this
file says *who it's behaving for.* When a copy or design decision is close, ask which
avatar it serves and whether it moves them one step down the funnel.

> **On the names below.** MK supplied these as real reference points — people whose
> world he wants the site to land in. They are used here as **anchors for a type**,
> not as researched profiles: nothing about any individual's employment, taste,
> opinions, or history is asserted, because inventing that would make this file
> worse than useless. Everything written under each avatar is about *what someone in
> that role needs from a site like this* — which is generalizable and safe to build
> against. The blanks marked **`[MK: ...]`** are the parts only MK can fill in, and
> they're where the real value is.

---

## The shared truth

Every avatar is doing the same thing in the first fifteen seconds: **deciding whether
this person is interesting enough to keep reading.** They are not evaluating a
résumé. They're checking for a point of view.

They all bounce for the same three reasons:

1. **It reads as a portfolio template.** Grid of thumbnails, "Selected Works",
   generic serif. Signals: this person is presenting, not making.
2. **It asks before it gives.** A contact CTA above the fold, a newsletter popup, a
   form. Signals: this person wants something from me.
3. **It's ambiguous about what he actually does.** If they can't answer "what is this
   guy" by the end of the first screen, they leave.

They all convert on the same thing: **a distinct voice attached to finished work.**

## 1. The Creative Director
**Anchor:** Jim Morrissey

**What they're scanning for.** Taste and judgment — whether MK's instincts would
survive contact with a brief. They read the *writing* more carefully than anyone
else on this list, because writing is where taste is hardest to fake.

**What makes them bounce.** Work with no thinking attached to it. A reel with no
explanation of the idea underneath is just footage.

**What the site must prove.** That the point of view is consistent across projects —
that OutroSpective, How to Meet People, and Porsche Parlor are recognizably the same
person asking the same question in different rooms.

**Entry point:** an essay. **Next step:** the timeline, then reach out.

**`[MK: what's the one piece of work you'd want this person to see first, and why?]`**

## 2. The Director
**Anchor:** Adam Donald

**What they're scanning for.** Whether MK can hold a frame and hold a subject.
Directors read craft fast — one badly-composed photo costs more credibility here
than anywhere else on this list.

**What makes them bounce.** Beautiful images with nothing behind them. A director
has seen ten thousand nice landscapes; they're looking for the shot that required a
decision.

**What the site must prove.** That MK gets people to say true things on camera. That
is the actual skill the travel series is built on, and it's the hardest thing to
demonstrate in stills — which is what the livid embeds are for.

**Entry point:** the hero film or a livid embed. **Next step:** the timeline, then
reach out.

**`[MK: which single clip best shows you getting a real answer out of someone?]`**

**Anchors:** Windows95man · Janne Vartia · Kim Kopenen

**What they're scanning for.** Whether MK is fun. Whether the work has any play in
it. This is the avatar most allergic to earnestness and most responsive to a wink.

**What makes them bounce.** Self-seriousness. A site that presents its author as an
Artist rather than a person who makes things.

**What the site must prove.** That the humor is real and load-bearing — the karaoke

decoration for this avatar; they're the qualifying signal.

**Entry point:** the home page. **Next step:** an essay, then reach out.

**`[MK: is this avatar someone you want to work WITH, or someone whose audience you

## 4. The Producer
**Anchors:** Tara LeMasters · producers at 100 Prcnt (Amsterdam) and comparable
production companies

**What they're scanning for.** Can this person be trusted with a budget and a
schedule. Producers are the most conversion-ready avatar on this list and the most
poorly served by the site as it stands — they need evidence of *delivery*, not just
taste.

**What makes them bounce.** No signal that anything has ever actually shipped. Also:
no obvious way to get in touch once they've decided.

**What the site must prove.** That MK has run real productions with real stakes.
The line already on the home page — *"In a previous life, I executive produced
commercial campaigns for some of the biggest ad agencies in the world"* — is the
single highest-value sentence on the site for this avatar, and it's currently buried
inside an overlay.

**Entry point:** direct link from an email or LinkedIn. **Next step:** reach out —
this avatar can skip the funnel.

**`[MK: which campaigns/agencies can be named publicly? Named work converts this
avatar; "some of the biggest" does not.]`**

## How to use this file

**Before building anything, name the avatar.** A change that serves all four usually
serves none — the version that lands hard with the Producer is not the version that
lands with Windows95man.

**Ranking, for when they conflict:** Producer and Creative Director first (they hire),
Director second (they collaborate), Artist third (they amplify). The home page is
allowed to lead with personality because personality is what qualifies MK for all
four — but every path out of it should reach proof-of-work within two clicks.

**The one-line test.** After any change, read the screen as each avatar and ask:
*would this person's next click be the one I intended?* If two avatars would click
different things, the section is doing too much.
